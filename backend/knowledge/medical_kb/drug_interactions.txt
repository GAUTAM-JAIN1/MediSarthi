MEDISARTHI — GENERAL DRUG INTERACTION REFERENCE
(Shared knowledge base for MedicineInteractionCrew. General reference facts only —
not exhaustive, not a substitute for a pharmacist/doctor. Used to ground the
interaction_checker agent's answers, not to replace its own reasoning.)

== ANTICOAGULANTS / ANTIPLATELETS ==

Warfarin + Aspirin (or other NSAIDs like Ibuprofen, Diclofenac): Both increase
bleeding risk through different mechanisms (Warfarin blocks clotting factors,
NSAIDs affect platelet function and can irritate the GI lining). Combined use
significantly raises risk of serious bleeding, especially GI bleeds. Generally
classified Major to Contraindicated depending on dose/duration. Requires close
doctor supervision if unavoidable.

Warfarin + Antibiotics (Ciprofloxacin, Metronidazole, many others): Several
common antibiotics inhibit the liver enzymes that clear Warfarin, raising INR
and bleeding risk. Usually needs more frequent INR monitoring during and after
antibiotic course, not necessarily avoidance.

Aspirin + Clopidogrel: Often prescribed together intentionally after cardiac
events (dual antiplatelet therapy) under doctor supervision — the combination
itself is a known, monitored protocol, not automatically dangerous, but
increases bleeding risk versus either drug alone and should never be
self-initiated without a cardiologist's direction.

== SEROTONERGIC DRUGS ==

SSRIs (Fluoxetine, Sertraline, Escitalopram, etc.) + MAOIs (Phenelzine,
Tranylcypromine): Can cause serotonin syndrome — a potentially
life-threatening condition (agitation, high fever, rapid heart rate, muscle
rigidity). This combination is classically Contraindicated; label guidance
typically requires a washout period of at least 2 weeks between stopping one
and starting the other.

SSRIs + Tramadol: Tramadol itself has serotonergic activity; combining with
an SSRI raises serotonin syndrome risk. Usually classified Moderate to Major
depending on doses involved.

SSRIs + NSAIDs: Both can independently increase bleeding risk (SSRIs affect
platelet serotonin uptake); combined use raises GI bleeding risk, particularly
in older adults. Typically Moderate.

== BLOOD PRESSURE / HEART MEDICINES ==

ACE inhibitors (Enalapril, Ramipril, Lisinopril) + Potassium supplements or
potassium-sparing diuretics (Spironolactone): Can cause dangerously high
potassium (hyperkalemia), leading to heart rhythm problems. Needs monitoring
of blood potassium if combined; not automatically avoided but requires
oversight.

Beta-blockers (Atenolol, Metoprolol) + Verapamil/Diltiazem (calcium channel
blockers): Both slow heart rate and reduce contraction strength; combined use
can cause dangerously slow heart rate or worsen heart failure. Usually
Moderate to Major, dose-dependent.

== DIABETES MEDICINES ==

Metformin + Contrast dye (used in some imaging scans): Rare risk of lactic
acidosis if kidney function is reduced around the time of contrast imaging.
Doctors typically pause Metformin briefly around such procedures — this is a
procedural interaction, not a general everyday-use one.

Sulfonylureas (Glimepiride, Glipizide) + Alcohol: Alcohol can unpredictably
raise or lower blood sugar and mask hypoglycemia symptoms; combined use raises
risk of dangerous low blood sugar episodes.

== COMMON OTC / EVERYDAY COMBINATIONS ==

Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) + Alcohol (regular, heavy use): Both are processed
by the liver; regular heavy alcohol use alongside frequent Paracetamol use
raises risk of liver damage. Occasional standard-dose Paracetamol with
moderate alcohol is generally considered low risk; the concern is chronic
heavy combined use.

Antacids (containing Calcium, Magnesium, or Aluminum) + many antibiotics
(Tetracyclines, Fluoroquinolones like Ciprofloxacin): Antacids can bind the
antibiotic in the gut and reduce absorption significantly. Usually managed by
simply spacing doses at least 2 hours apart — a timing interaction, not a
true contraindication.

Multivitamins/Iron supplements + Levothyroxine (thyroid medicine): Calcium,
iron, and some multivitamins reduce Levothyroxine absorption if taken close
together. Standard guidance is to separate doses by several hours, usually
taking Levothyroxine on an empty stomach in the morning.

== GENERAL NOTES FOR THE AGENT ==

- "None" severity should still be considered whenever two medicines have no
  well-established interaction — do not invent a concern where none exists.
- Herbal/ayurvedic supplements (e.g., St. John's Wort) can interact with many
  prescription drugs (notably reducing effectiveness of birth control,
  antidepressants, and blood thinners via liver enzyme induction) — worth
  flagging if mentioned, even though this file focuses on conventional
  medicines.
- This file is a grounding reference, not a complete pharmacology database.
  The agent should still rely on its own trained medical knowledge for pairs
  not covered here, and should say so honestly (classify as uncertain rather
  than invent a specific mechanism) when a pair is genuinely unfamiliar.

  == DUPLICATE THERAPY / SAME DRUG UNDER DIFFERENT BRAND NAMES ==

Common Indian OTC brand names that are the SAME active ingredient, not separate
medicines. Taking two of these together is not a "drug interaction" — it is
double-dosing the same drug, which risks overdose:

Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) — Dolo 650, Crocin, Calpol, Paracip, P-500, Fepanil
Ibuprofen — Brufen, Combiflam (also contains Paracetamol)
Cetirizine — Cetzine, Alerid, Okacet
Domperidone — Domstal, Vomistop
Pantoprazole — Pantocid, Pan-D (also contains Domperidone)

If a patient's list includes two brands mapping to the same active ingredient,
flag as Duplicate Therapy (see agent instructions), not as a standard interaction.
