MEDISARTHI — STANDARD LAB TEST REFERENCE RANGES
(Shared knowledge base for LabReportCrew. General adult reference ranges from
widely-used clinical lab conventions — actual "normal" ranges vary slightly by
lab, method, age, sex, and pregnancy status. This file grounds the
lab_report_analyst agent's plain-language explanations; the report's own
printed reference range should always take priority over this file when
present, since it reflects the specific lab's method.)

== COMPLETE BLOOD COUNT (CBC) ==

Haemoglobin: Adult male ~13.5–17.5 g/dL, adult female ~12.0–15.5 g/dL.
Low = anemia (fatigue, pallor common causes: iron deficiency, blood loss,
chronic disease). High = polycythemia, dehydration, or living at altitude.

WBC (White Blood Cell count): ~4,000–11,000 cells/µL. High = infection,
inflammation, stress response, or (rarely) blood disorders. Low = certain
viral infections, some medications, bone marrow issues.

Platelet count: ~150,000–450,000/µL. Low = increased bleeding/bruising risk
(causes: viral infection like dengue, some medications, autoimmune conditions).
High = can occur with inflammation, iron deficiency, or after major bleeding.

== BLOOD SUGAR ==

Fasting Blood Sugar: Normal ~70–99 mg/dL. Prediabetes ~100–125 mg/dL.
Diabetes ≥126 mg/dL (on repeat testing).

Post-Prandial (2hr after meal): Normal <140 mg/dL. Prediabetes 140–199 mg/dL.
Diabetes ≥200 mg/dL.

HbA1c (average 3-month blood sugar): Normal <5.7%. Prediabetes 5.7–6.4%.
Diabetes ≥6.5%.

== LIPID PROFILE ==

Total Cholesterol: Desirable <200 mg/dL. Borderline 200–239. High ≥240.

LDL ("bad" cholesterol): Optimal <100 mg/dL. Near-optimal 100–129.
Borderline high 130–159. High 160–189. Very high ≥190.

HDL ("good" cholesterol): Low (higher risk) if <40 mg/dL (men) or <50 mg/dL
(women). Higher HDL is generally protective.

Triglycerides: Normal <150 mg/dL. Borderline high 150–199. High 200–499.
Very high ≥500 (raises pancreatitis risk).

== THYROID FUNCTION ==

TSH: Normal ~0.4–4.0 mIU/L (some labs use 0.5–5.0). High TSH usually means
underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism); low TSH usually means overactive
thyroid (hyperthyroidism) — the direction is often counterintuitive to
patients, worth explaining plainly.

Free T4: Normal ~0.8–1.8 ng/dL. Interpreted alongside TSH, not alone.

== KIDNEY FUNCTION ==

Creatinine: Adult male ~0.7–1.3 mg/dL, adult female ~0.6–1.1 mg/dL. High
suggests reduced kidney function; needs context (hydration, muscle mass,
medications like NSAIDs can also raise it).

Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN): ~7–20 mg/dL. High can indicate reduced kidney
function, dehydration, or high protein intake.

== LIVER FUNCTION ==

ALT (SGPT): ~7–56 U/L. AST (SGOT): ~10–40 U/L. Elevation suggests liver
cell stress/damage — causes range from fatty liver, alcohol, viral hepatitis,
to certain medications.

Bilirubin (Total): ~0.1–1.2 mg/dL. High causes yellowing of skin/eyes
(jaundice) — causes include liver disease, bile duct blockage, or increased
red blood cell breakdown.

== VITAMIN LEVELS ==

Vitamin D (25-OH): Deficient <20 ng/mL. Insufficient 20–29 ng/mL.
Sufficient 30–100 ng/mL. Very common deficiency in the general population,
usually not urgent but worth addressing.

Vitamin B12: Normal ~200–900 pg/mL (varies by lab). Low levels linked to
fatigue, nerve symptoms (tingling, numbness), and in chronic cases anemia.

== GENERAL NOTES FOR THE AGENT ==

- Always prefer the normal_range printed directly on the report over these
  general figures when both are available — the report's range reflects the
  actual lab's equipment/method and is more accurate for that specific result.
- These are general adult ranges; pediatric, pregnancy, and elderly reference
  ranges can differ meaningfully and are not covered in this file.
- A single abnormal value in isolation is often less meaningful than a
  pattern across several related tests — encourage the "next_steps" field to
  reflect that nuance rather than reacting to one number in isolation.
